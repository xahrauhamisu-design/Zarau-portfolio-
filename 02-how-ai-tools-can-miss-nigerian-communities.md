# How AI Tools Can Miss the Mark in Nigerian Communities

*This is my personal view, based on using AI tools in my own work. It is not a research study.*

AI tools are often sold as useful for everyone. In my experience, they work best for the people
they were built around. Here are four ways I think they can miss the mark in places like Nigeria.

## 1. They can answer for the wrong audience

When I used ChatGPT for a research summary about a Nigerian topic, the answer was fluent but written
as if for an academic reader in a richer country. It used ideas and wording that did not fit the
setting I was working in. It only became useful after I rewrote my prompt with the audience, the
place and the language level. If I had not noticed, I would have used it as it was.

## 2. Language support is uneven

Many AI tools are strongest in English. In my experience, they are often weaker in languages like
Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo, which millions of people speak every day. A tool that works well in English
but poorly in the language people actually use at home leaves many of them out.

## 3. Cost and connection matter

An AI tool that needs a fast, always-on internet connection is hard to use where data is expensive
and networks drop. Tools that can run with low data, or offline, would reach more people.

## 4. A fluent answer can feel more reliable than it is

AI answers are written smoothly, so people may trust them without checking. This is risky when the
topic is health, money or the law, where a wrong answer can hurt someone.

## What could help

- **Community review.** Ask local people to check AI answers before a tool is used with their community.
- **Local examples.** Build and test tools with local languages, names, places and situations.
- **Small, low-data tools.** Offer simple options that work on a basic phone with a weak connection.
- **Plain-language guidance.** Teach users how to question an answer and check it.

## Closing thought

The question I ask about any AI tool is simple: *who was this built for, and who was left out?*
For AI to help in Nigerian communities, the people there need to help shape it.
